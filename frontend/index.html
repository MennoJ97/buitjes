About Buitjes

Rainfall over the Netherlands in 5-minute steps: an hour of measured radar behind you, six hours of forecast ahead. Every number on this page comes from this server, which fetches and reduces the weather data itself; the only thing the browser asks anyone else for is the basemap under the rain.

Reading the timeline

Observed
What the radar actually measured.
Nowcast
First two hours ahead, mostly radar extrapolation.
Forecast
Beyond that, increasingly the weather model.

The forecast is seamless — that split is a reading aid, not a change of data source. Accuracy drops the further right you scrub.

A step badged Estimated is one KNMI published empty: all twenty runs identical and the country dry in the middle of a rain band. Rather than draw that, this stands in for it with the average of the steps five minutes either side, and says so — on the map as the badge, on a chart as a dashed line.

How sure is it?

The forecast is twenty runs, not one. What the map paints — Expected on the switch — is the twenty pooled into a single field: their average decides where the rain is, and the runs' own range of intensities decides how hard it falls, so a shower keeps its edges instead of being smeared across twenty guesses at its position. But where a shower lands is the thing twenty runs disagree about most, long before they disagree that one is coming at all.

Low and High above the colour bar redraw the map as the dry and wet ends of that disagreement: the rate that all but two of the runs beat, and the one only two of them reach. The gap between them is how much the ensemble is arguing. Hover anywhere to read all three at once.

Both are taken over a small radius rather than a single square kilometre, which is deliberate: at one kilometre the low end reads zero almost everywhere, because a shower two streets away counts as a miss. So read them as near here rather than exactly here.

None of this exists over the measured hour: radar is one number, not twenty, and a measurement has no percentiles to take. Scrub back into it and the map shows what was measured whichever end you have selected, and the readout says measured rather than naming a band.

Data & weather models

Precipitation comes from the KNMI Data Platform, licensed CC BY 4.0.

    • Open-Meteo temperature, wind, solar, rain outlook

      The KNMI product is precipitation only, so everything else comes from Open-Meteo's ensemble endpoint — which returns every member, giving the same kind of spread the rain forecast has. It also carries the rain outlook past KNMI's six hours, hourly instead of every five minutes. Fetched server-side.

    Mapping

    • MapLibre GL JS renderer

      Open-source GPU map rendering, served from this host rather than a CDN.

    • OpenFreeMap tiles

      OpenStreetMap vector tiles, free and without a key. Four of the five basemaps come from here: their Dark and Positron styles as they are, plus two more built from Dark — the high-contrast one recoloured here for quiet roads and white place names on a heavy halo, and a plain one with the names taken off altogether. Because the names are real text rather than part of a picture, the rain slides underneath them instead of over them.

    • OpenStreetMap standard tiles tiles

      The familiar OSM rendering, served by the OpenStreetMap Foundation. One image with the names drawn into it, so on this style they sit under the rain rather than over it.

    How it works

    • Push, not polling. A Python service subscribes to KNMI's MQTT notification service and ingests each file as it is announced, rather than repeatedly asking whether one has appeared.
    • Two grids, one raster. The forecast arrives as NetCDF on a lat/lon grid and the radar as KNMI HDF5 on a polar stereographic one; both are resampled onto a single Web Mercator raster so the map can stretch one canvas over them.
    • 16-bit WebP packing. Each rain rate is split across the red and green channels of a lossless WebP, and blue flags the pixels no radar measured — so a frame of the whole domain is around 30 KB and a full 6-hour cycle a couple of megabytes.
    • Colour on the GPU. A WebGL shader recombines those bytes and applies the colour scale, so frames can be recoloured without refetching. A Rust server hands out the finished frames.
